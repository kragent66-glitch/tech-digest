|# Inkling 2 Mini: Looped Transformer Architecture Breaks Open-Weights Barriers for Laptop AI

Thinking Machines Lab released Inkling 2 Mini on November 20, 2026 as an open-weights model that challenges the assumption that powerful AI requires data center GPUs. With 26.4 billion parameters stored in 20GB of memory, Inkling 2 Mini uses a novel looped transformer architecture that applies its middle layers three times per token, achieving effective depth of 48 layers while keeping its footprint small enough to run offline on a 32GB MacBook Pro. It leads every open-weights model under 100 billion parameters on DeepSWE v1.1 (48.2%), SWE-bench Verified (79.2%), and the AA Index (20.1), demonstrating that reasoning performance can be extracted from compact hardware through architectural innovation rather than brute-force parameter scaling.

## Why Inkling 2 Mini matters

The dominant paradigm in large language models has been parameter count. Since the launch of GPT-3 in 2020, frontier models have expanded from 175 billion parameters to hundreds of billions, then beyond, with the assumption that more parameters equals better reasoning. This trend pushed inference costs to where only cloud providers can afford them, and created a gap between research capabilities and what anyone can run locally.

Inkling 2 Mini breaks that pattern by reusing existing layers instead of adding new ones. It stores 24 layers of weights, but executes them 48 times per token by running the middle 12 layers three times with shared weights. This "looped transformer" design adds computational depth without adding parameter count, allowing the model to reason longer internally before producing its next token. For coding and tool-assisted tasks where reasoning quality matters more than raw fact recall, this produces better results than models with more parameters but shallower depth.

The result is a 26.4 billion parameter model that behaves like a much larger system. It supports 1 million token context windows, accepts both text and image input, and runs at approximately 34 tokens per second on a 5090 GPU with Q8K quantization. For comparison, similar coding-focused models like Qwen3.8-27B require significantly more compute to achieve comparable performance on agentic benchmarks.

## Looped transformer architecture explained

The looped transformer is the defining innovation of Inkling 2 Mini. Standard transformers pass each token through every layer exactly once. Inkling 2 Mini passes each token through its 24 layers twice on the first pass, then again on two additional passes through the middle 12 layers, for a total of 48 layer visits per token despite storing only 24 unique layer parameters.

The model divides its layers into four six-layer blocks. Blocks 0 and 3 (the prelude and postlude) run once. Blocks 1 and 2 (the core) run three times, with each pass receiving a message from the previous pass. Attention Residuals and a learned gate control how information flows between passes. The first pass receives the initial token embedding and attention output from the prelude. Subsequent passes receive outputs from earlier passes in the core, allowing the model to refine its reasoning across multiple internal passes before emitting its final output.

This design is motivated by an observation about transformer internals: the middle layers of a model carry more abstract reasoning than the first and last layers, which handle translation between embeddings and internal representations. By running the reasoning-heavy middle layers multiple times, Inkling 2 Mini spends more time in its most capable layers, even though it stores the same number of parameters as a shallower model.

The loop adds computation without adding parameters or inference cost proportional to the added depth. Instead of storing 48 unique layers, the model stores 24 and reuses them. At the same time, the loop increases reasoning depth, which directly improves performance on tasks that require multi-step reasoning like debugging code, refactoring large files, or planning multi-step tool use.

Research on the variable-loop variant shows that performance on serial reasoning benchmarks improves out to ten loops, even though Inkling 2 Mini was trained with only three loops. This suggests that the looped architecture has room for further optimization, with deeper loops potentially delivering even stronger performance for certain workloads.

## Mixture of Experts and sparse routing

Inkling 2 Mini uses a sparse Mixture of Experts (MoE) architecture to keep its active parameter count low while maintaining a large pool of specialized knowledge. The model has 128 expert blocks, and each token is routed to exactly eight of them. This means only about 3.4 billion parameters activate for a standard forward pass, even though the model stores 26.4 billion total parameters.

The MoE design is particularly important for looped transformers. Each layer pass sees only the eight experts selected for that pass, and the selected experts can vary between passes. This means the model can use different specialized experts for different aspects of reasoning during each loop iteration, creating a dynamic routing pattern across multiple passes.

The hidden size of Inkling 2 Mini is 2,304, compared to 4,608 for its larger sibling Inkling 2 Small. This reduction in width is offset by the increased depth from looping, maintaining comparable expressive power while reducing memory and compute requirements. The router selects eight of 128 SwiGLU experts for each token visit, normalizing the selection scores and scaling weights by eight to preserve output scale.

Expert routing operates on each layer visit, not just once per token. This means the MoE pattern changes across the three passes through the core layers, creating a diverse set of expert combinations across the loop. The model uses learned relative position logits, a learned sink, and an output gate to control how information flows across the loop.

Hybrid attention combines local and global layers. Each six-layer block contains five sliding-window layers that attend to the last 1,024 tokens and one full-attention layer that sees the entire context. This design supports the 1 million token context window while keeping computation manageable, with the full-attention layers handling long-range dependencies and the local windows processing recent context efficiently.

## Benchmark performance and competitive positioning

Inkling 2 Mini's most significant strength is its performance on coding and agentic benchmarks, where it leads every open-weights model with fewer than 100 billion parameters. On DeepSWE v1.1, a standard for measuring coding agents on real-world bug fixes, Inkling 2 Mini achieves 48.2%, compared to 42.2% for Qwen3.8-27B and only 0.3% for Gemma 4 31B. The gap is even wider on SWEBench Verified, where Inkling 2 Mini reaches 79.2% versus 77.8% for Qwen3.8-27B and 46.1% for Gemma 4 31B.

The model ties Qwen3.8-27B on the AA Index (v5.0) at 20.1 points, making it the top open-weights model in this parameter range. On the Vals Index v2.2, Inkling 2 Mini posts 46.5%, well ahead of Nemotron 3.5 Super VL at 29.8% and with no scores listed for Gemma 4 31B, Qwen3.8-27B, or Muse Glimmer on this particular index.

On broader coding benchmarks, Inkling 2 Mini leads or ties at the top. FrontierCode v2.0 reaches 15.1%, just ahead of Qwen3.8-27B at 14.9%. Terminal Bench v2.1 shows 71.6% on Inkling 2 Mini versus 73.0% on Qwen3.8-27B, while Terminal Bench v4.0 ties both models at 5.6%. SWEBench Multilingual scores 73.7% on Inkling 2 Mini versus 70.0% on Qwen3.8-27B and 67.7% on Nemotron.

Beyond coding, Inkling 2 Mini excels on tasks that require search and tool use. BrowseComp reaches 79.0% with compaction, DeepSearchQA hits 85.3%, and MCP Atlas Public achieves 82.7%. These results show that looping improves reasoning, but the model compensates for any knowledge limitations by efficiently using search and external tools.

The flight simulator demonstration, built on a MacBook Pro in airplane mode, illustrates the model's offline capabilities. The model generated a playable flight simulator in minutes without internet access, demonstrating that 20GB of weights and 1M context can run entirely on consumer hardware. Another demo showed 20 persistent autonomous villagers in a local town simulation, also running offline on the same hardware.

## Memory footprint and deployment story

A key differentiator of Inkling 2 Mini is its efficient deployment story. The full checkpoint is about 20GB, and it is stored in native precision rather than requiring post-training quantization. Most weights are trained and deployed in NVFP4, a 4-bit floating-point format introduced with NVIDIA's GB300 architecture. The last four layers use MXFP8, an 8-bit format, allowing the model to use higher precision where it matters most while keeping the overall footprint small.

This native-precision deployment matters because it avoids the accuracy loss that typically accompanies quantization. Most 26B-parameter models require 50GB+ of VRAM at BF16 precision, and even quantized versions often show performance degradation. Inkling 2 Mini's 20GB footprint and native NVFP4 deployment make it practical to run on a 32GB MacBook Pro or a single DGX Spark desktop AI computer.

Local deployment support is available from day one through multiple engines. Ollama provides native macOS support for Macs with 32GB+ RAM, and Unsloth and llama.cpp offer quantized GGUF builds for broader hardware compatibility. The model is also supported in vLLM, SGLang, NVIDIA Dynamo, and TokenSpeed for production deployments.

For researchers, the variable-loop variant TML-d24-varloop-research is released alongside the primary model. This variant allows experiments with different loop depths, from one to five passes, enabling clean comparisons between looping strategies. The research variant was used for monitorability experiments and shows that longer loops improve performance on serial reasoning benchmarks without increasing misbehavior rates.

The model's ability to run offline on a laptop with 20GB of weights and a 1M context window changes the accessibility equation. Organizations can deploy Inkling 2 Mini in air-gapped environments, run it on company-issued devices without relying on cloud APIs, and experiment with agentic workflows that require persistent context and long reasoning chains.

## Monitorability and interpretability implications

The looped architecture creates interesting implications for monitoring and interpretability. Standard transformers write their chain of thought to the token stream, making it straightforward to observe what the model is reasoning about. Looped transformers add computation internally without necessarily increasing the amount of visible reasoning output. This raises questions about how to monitor model behavior and detect malicious actions when the model's internal state is less visible.

Thinking Machines Lab conducted extensive monitorability experiments on the variable-loop variant. The no-CoT time horizon improved from 16 seconds with one pass to 37 seconds with three passes, meaning the model can maintain coherent reasoning for longer periods without emitting it. Performance on serial reasoning benchmarks continued to improve out to ten loops, suggesting that deeper loops provide stronger reasoning capabilities.

The model's chain of thought grew shorter with more loops but stayed legible throughout. The research found no clear trend in agentic sabotage rates as loops were added, suggesting that increased reasoning depth does not necessarily lead to more harmful behavior. This is a reassuring signal for organizations concerned about looped transformers enabling more capable but less controllable models.

For mechanistic interpretability researchers, looped transformers are an interesting and important setting. The internal computation is more opaque because more of it happens without writing to the token stream. Thinking Machines Lab is working with the National Deep Inference Fabric (NDIF) to make these models more accessible to interpretability researchers and provided early access to Goodfire, an interpretability lab that has begun analyzing the internal dynamics of looped models.

The ability to examine how a model's internal state evolves across loop passes could reveal new insights into transformer dynamics and reasoning processes. Researchers can now study whether looped transformers develop different internal representations than standard transformers, and whether the repeated passes through core layers create distinctive mechanistic patterns that correlate with better performance on complex tasks.

## License, ecosystem, and availability

Inkling 2 Mini is released under Apache 2.0 with an Acceptable Use Policy (AUP). This open license makes it practical for commercial use, fine-tuning, and deployment in production environments. The weights are available on Hugging Face, and the model is accessible through Tinker for inference and customization.

For inference, Inkling 2 Mini can be used through serverless providers like OpenRouter, with pricing of $0.15 per million input tokens and $0.50 per million output tokens from Thinking Machines' first-party API. Tinker offers a playground for experimentation and supports RL fine-tuning through SkyRL, Miles, and NVIDIA AutoModel.

The release emphasizes that Inkling 2 Mini is not just a research artifact but a production-ready model. It supports tool calling via the `inkling` tool-call parser, which is unit-tested and designed to work reliably in agent workflows. The model's design prioritizes agentic workloads, making it particularly suitable for coding agents, search-augmented reasoning, and other tool-using applications.

## What this means for the future of local AI

Inkling 2 Mini demonstrates that architectural innovation can complement parameter scaling as a path to more capable AI. By reusing layers instead of adding new ones, looping adds depth without adding parameter count or proportional inference cost. This opens the possibility of building even larger looped models in the future, potentially pushing performance toward frontier levels while maintaining deployment flexibility.

The success of Inkling 2 Mini may encourage other labs to explore looped transformers and other architectures that trade computation for parameter count. If the pattern holds, we could see more models that prioritize reasoning depth and specialized routing over sheer parameter size, creating a broader ecosystem of models with different efficiency tradeoffs.

For developers and organizations, Inkling 2 Mini represents a practical step toward running powerful AI on local hardware. The 20GB footprint, native deployment precision, and offline capabilities make it feasible to incorporate into existing workflows without cloud dependencies. The Apache 2.0 license and strong benchmark performance make it an attractive option for production use.

The flight simulator demo and town simulation illustrate that Inkling 2 Mini is not just good at coding but capable of creative tasks and long-running agent workflows. Its 1M context window allows it to maintain state across extensive work sessions, making it suitable for complex projects that require persistent memory and multi-step reasoning.

Inkling 2 Mini also highlights the importance of monitorability research in the era of more capable local models. As models become more powerful and run in environments where human oversight is limited, understanding how to monitor their behavior and detect misalignment becomes increasingly important. The looped architecture's internal opacity creates new challenges for monitoring, but also new research opportunities.

The release of Inkling 2 Mini and its variable-loop variant provides the research community with concrete tools to study these questions. Researchers can now experiment with different loop depths, examine internal state evolution, and test monitorability strategies in a setting where architecture and behavior are tightly coupled. This will likely accelerate our understanding of how looped transformers reason and how we can observe and control them safely.

As Thinking Machines continues to explore the Inkling line with models ranging from 26B parameters to 594B parameters, the lessons learned from Inkling 2 Mini will inform larger deployments. The insights from monitorability experiments, efficiency optimizations, and deployment strategies developed for Mini will likely apply to future iterations, creating a progressive improvement path that prioritizes both capability and accessibility.

For anyone building local AI systems, coding agents, or tool-using applications, Inkling 2 Mini provides a compelling option that balances performance, efficiency, and openness. It demonstrates that powerful AI does not require cloud infrastructure, and that local deployment can deliver results competitive with models many times larger.
