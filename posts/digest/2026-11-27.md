### 1Password: Attacker Pushed Malicious Code Into Backend, Vaults Stayed Sealed
1Password says an intruder got into an employee's corporate GitHub account on November 19, used it to read source code and push malicious code into backend services, then copied customer names, sign-in email addresses and non-secret account metadata for a subset of users. The company stresses that vault contents and the keys protecting them were never exposed, since 1Password cannot read them. Containment, a clean service rebuild and internal credential rotation followed, and the disclosure landed November 25. The takeaway for teams: the weakest link here was a GitHub identity, not the encryption model itself.
[1Password](https://1password.com/blog/update-on-nov-2026-security-incident) | [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50644817)

### NYT Tells Freelance Photographers to Skip Photoshop 2027 Over AI Metadata
The New York Times has instructed contributing photographers to avoid Adobe Photoshop 2027 (release 28.0.0 and later) while on assignment. In an email seen by The Verge, photo leaders Meaghan Looram and Josh Haner warned that the November update applies AI "in ways that may not be apparent," and that using it "can result in metadata identifying photographs as AI-generated." Adobe has been rolling out C2PA content credentials across its apps since August. The Times, which permits only minimal colour adjustment, wants no AI tags anywhere near its journalism. Adobe and the Times declined to say which tools triggered the ban.

### Google Forces AI Agents Onto Workspace MCP Servers, and Gmail Still Cannot Send
On November 24 Google made eight Workspace MCP servers generally available and added a clause to its API terms: from February 1, 2027, any tool "driven by an AI Agent" must reach Gmail, Drive and Calendar through those servers. The catch is capability. Gmail's server can search, read threads, create drafts and apply labels, but cannot send, reply, forward or edit a draft, because those endpoints "require partner access and aren't generally available." Agents hitting REST directly get Standard-tier quotas regardless of paid tier. Google also caps retention of embeddings, memory indexes and profiles built from Workspace data at 30 days.
[Tech Stackups](https://techstackups.com/articles/google-workspace-ai-agents-mcp/) | [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50653477)

### OpenAI's Astra 6.2 and Sol 6.2 Look Cost-Tuned, and They Underthink
Independent analysis built on the Agent Duel leaderboard argues OpenAI's Astra 6.2 and Sol 6.2 releases were tuned for cost rather than capability. Compared with matched opponents on the same tasks, both models reason and emit far fewer tokens than their predecessors: Astra 6 and Sol 6.1 look normal, while the 6.2 pair are outliers. The effect is statistically solid, with p=0.00013 for Sol's reasoning drop and 0.0064 for Astra's, and output-token drops at 0.0055 and 0.022. Reviewers panned the releases. Cheaper inference per task helps budgets, less so when the model stops thinking through hard problems.
[micjm](https://micjm.com/blog/openai-is-in-cost-control-mode/) | [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50652527)

### An AMD Patch Set Lifts llama.cpp to 55 Tokens/s on an R9700 eGPU
llama-cpp-rdna-boosts applies a minimal set of AMD-specific patches to a pinned llama.cpp commit and re-applies them as upstream moves. Writing on devnull.land, one user running a Swift-1.5-Qwen3.8-27B Q6_K_L model on an AMD R9700 over an eGPU link measured prefill rising from roughly 700 to 1,300 tokens per second, and generation from 35 to 55 tokens per second. The contrast is with newer "bespoke" forks that chase speed by changing how a model selects experts rather than by tuning hardware. Here the changeset stays small and inspectable.
[devnull.land](https://devnull.land/amd-specific-llama-cpp) | [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50657265)

### YouTube Ad Punisher Reloads the Page Every Time an Ad Starts
YouTube Ad Punisher is a browser extension that auto-reloads YouTube whenever an ad begins playing, on the theory that a fresh page load often skips the break. It is a blunt instrument, and the Hacker News thread spent hundreds of comments arguing about whether it works, what it costs creators, and how long the platform will tolerate it. The project sits in a long tradition of client-side protest against ad load. It is not ad blocking, it is reload roulette, and the repository makes no secret of that.

Works in Progress runs a photo essay on Kamil Khan Mumtaz, born in Calcutta in 1939, trained at London's Architectural Association, and practising in Pakistan since 1966. ### A Background-Independent Quantization of Gravity From Null Cones
A paper submitted to arXiv on November 25 proposes quantizing vacuum general relativity without a fixed background, working on intersecting null cones of a bulk subregion at finite Newton's constant. The authors promote the reduced phase space's Poisson brackets to commutators, build a nonperturbative Hilbert space, and obtain a Lorentzian path integral whose sums converge absolutely. As a check they compute graviton pair creation nonperturbatively and recover Bekenstein-Hawking entropy for marginally trapped surfaces, including the logarithmic correction Sen derived via Euclidean methods. It is theory, not engineering, but unusually concrete for quantum gravity.
[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34924) | [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50651863)

### FBI Sting Catches 'RansomwareHelp' CTO Allegedly Marking Up Ransoms
The CTO and co-founder of ransomwarehelp.com, Juan Ricardo Palacio Escobar, 52, has been indicted in Rhode Island for wire fraud. The site promised "100% data recovery" using proprietary cryptography and insisted it never paid threat actors. An FBI sting posing as both victim and ransomware crew allegedly caught the opposite: Palacio negotiated a decryptor for about $6,000 in bitcoin, then billed the fake victim $16,010 including a $2,750 diagnostic fee. A separate case reportedly saw a $438,880 quote to an accounting firm hit by LockBit. The pattern is desperate victims, technical fog and a markup.

### AI Removed the Friction That Made Self-Hosting Tiring
Jan-Lukas Else has self-hosted for years but admits his enthusiasm faded: maintaining what already ran was enough, and new experiments cost an evening of changelogs. His infrastructure lives in Git as Docker Compose files and scripts, which means coding agents can work on it like any other repository. That collapsed the cost of trying a new service or automating a chore, and he found himself tinkering again, hardware included. The post is a useful counterpoint to AI-doom framing: the same agents people fear are making small-scale digital sovereignty cheaper for hobbyists.
