Nvidia Reinstates Jensen Huang as CEO, Stock Jumps 26%

Nvidia's board reinstated Jensen Huang as CEO effective immediately on 23 November, and the stock rose more than 26% on the news. The board said the unanimous vote followed "a series of discussions and meetings" with Huang, and that it had received input from senior levels of the United States government, which it says is confident the reinstatement "poses no national security risk." Huang called the return the start of "a new industrial revolution" and thanked the board for its "confidence and deliberation through this process." CNBC was still updating at publication, so the terms of the return remain undisclosed. CNBC | HN discussion

GLM-5.4 Loops 28 Layers Twice at 750B, Targets 2T-Class Intelligence

Z.ai shipped GLM-5.4, its first flagship on a Loop Transformer: 28 layers of the GLM-5 base model run for two iterations, buying depth without new parameters. Three fixes made it deployable at 750B - per-token gates that stop activation norms blowing up across passes, a hybrid KV cache that reuses the first pass's sparse selection plus a sliding window for the second, and sparse-index reuse. Z.ai claims intelligence comparable to 2T - 3T parameter models, 200 tokens/s off-peak and 70 - 80 at peak, with output length down 10 - 14% on GPQA-Diamond and MMLU-Pro. Weights are not out yet. Z.ai | HN discussion

Ladybird Ships Its Alpha - A Browser Engine Built From Scratch, Not a Fork

Ladybird published its first alpha for macOS and Linux, a browser wrapped around an engine built from the ground up rather than forked from Chromium or Gecko. The team is blunt about the state of it: some sites will not work, it will crash, and there are no security promises today - researchers are explicitly invited to attack it. It ships as a rolling channel with automatic updates, so testers no longer need to build from source, and it exposes WebDriver plus the Firefox DevTools protocol for automated testing. Broken sites and crashes can be reported from inside the browser. Ladybird | HN discussion

One LoRA Per Harness: 39% to 55% on Terminal-Bench, Tokens Cut From 2.9K to 1.5K

Hugging Face showed that an RL-trained adapter, fitted inside the agent harness you actually run, beats the frozen base model on that same harness. With Qwen3.8-27B held fixed, a harness adapter lifted Terminal-Bench 2.1 pass@1 from 39% to 55% under pi while average token use fell from 2.9K to 1.5K per task - the model reasons less, calls fewer tools, and gets it right first time more often. Adapters were trained separately for pi 1.5.2, OpenCode 2.2.7 and Hermes Agent v0.22.6, then merged into one super adapter that runs unchanged under all three. Hugging Face

Anthropic Publishes a Lean 4 Proof That VP ≠ VNP Over Every Field

Anthropic's formal-math repository now hosts a claimed proof that VP is a proper subset of VNP over every field, and that VNP is not contained in the closure of VP: circuits for the Hamiltonian cycle polynomial - and for the permanent outside characteristic 2 - need more than m^(c(log m)^(1/3)) gates for all large m, with the same bound holding for approximating circuits. It does not settle P versus NP. Anthropic states an internal research Claude model discovered the result, paper and formalization were both written by models, and no human mathematician has checked the proof. GitHub | HN discussion

The pitch is a real-time layer over your services - compose, extend, observe, discover and react to every one of them - filed under agents, API, backend and developer-tools. Nearly two years of steady growth rather than one viral week is the signal worth reading here. GitHub

MemTensor/MemOS - Self-evolving memory for agents, 35% token savings claimed

MemOS bills itself as a self-evolving memory operating system for LLMs and agents: ultra-persistent memory, hybrid retrieval, and cross-task skill reuse, with the repo claiming 35.24% token savings and DeepSeek harness support. It lands in the busiest category of the year - agent memory - and differentiates on persistence and skill reuse rather than plain vector search over chat history. 12,492 stars in 16.6 months is about 751 a month, with a push yesterday. zilliztech/claude-context - Make the whole codebase the agent's context

claude-context is a code-search MCP server that makes an entire repository the context for a coding agent, instead of the handful of files someone remembered to open. It comes from Zilliz, the vector-database company behind Milvus, which explains the shape: chunk the codebase, index it, retrieve semantically, hand the agent only what matters. 12,722 stars in 17.6 months - about 722 a month - with a push 12 days ago. Kritt-ai/open-kritt - Agentic vulnerability research that validates before it reports

open-kritt is a self-hosted tool that orchestrates agents to find and then validate security issues in code, with bug-bounty workflows in its topic list. Validation is the word that matters: most agentic scanners stop at flagging a plausible bug, and false-positive rate is what kills adoption in real triage queues, so a pipeline that argues a finding is genuine before filing it is the actual product. 2,441 stars in 4.2 months is about 585 a month - modest against the hype-tier repos, but it is the newest entry here and pushed eight days ago. Javis603/token-monitor - Where your AI coding spend actually goes, 56+ tools

Fun Tech

NO SPEAKER Makes a MacBook Trackpad Talk Using Its Haptic Motor

NO SPEAKER drives the Force Touch trackpad's haptic motor up to 4,000 times a second, fast enough that the glass itself produces audible sound. macOS's own voice is analysed every 10 ms for its two strongest formants, loudness and pitch, then replayed as sine tones through the motor so it buzzes like a voice instead of whistling; two resonances beat three in a blind test. It is one Swift file with no dependencies, MIT licensed, reaching the private MultitouchSupport framework because Apple's public haptics API exposes three fixed taps. GitHub | HN discussion

win4wine Runs Windows XP's Real Explorer as the Linux Desktop Shell

win4wine puts Windows XP's genuine explorer.exe on top of a patched Wine and uses it as the Linux desktop shell: Luna taskbar, Start menu, desktop and folder windows, with Linux applications wearing XP frames and appearing under All Programs. Tray icons bridge StatusNotifierItem and XEmbed, the XP network flyout reads NetworkManager, and DPI scaling plus Japanese input through fcitx5 both work. Installation is a script on a fresh Debian 13 using your own XP SP3 ISO - Microsoft binaries are not bundled. ReactOS and Windows 7 shells are options too. Explicitly experimental. GitHub | HN discussion

Spelunkr: 37 Channels of Forgotten Internet Archive Tapes, Live 24/7

Spelunkr is a free alternative TV network assembled entirely from the Internet Archive's forgotten videos: 37 channels and counting, 4,360 hours of programming, always live in 24/7 sync. The lineup is genuinely strange - a five-hour All in the Family marathon, Unsolved Mysteries blocks recorded in 1992, 1994 and 1999, channels named ORANGE SODA and FALLOUT SHELTER. No account, no ads, no checkout; every tape streams from archive.org, and viewers can suggest channels or report broken ones. Funding is a Ko-fi tip jar and a donate link to the Archive. Spelunkr | HN discussion

Arm's Lawyers Kill the Best AArch64 Reference on 4 December

The searchable AArch64 reference covering Armv8-A and Armv9-A instructions and system registers - the one developers reach for because Arm's official material is a PDF maze - shuts down on 4 December 2026. The maintainer, who signs himself Jon, says Arm's lawyers notified him to take the site down or face legal action, and thanked "all of the fans of this site over the years (even the ones at Arm)." He says he will look into solutions that could keep bookmarks and links working. The search interface is WebAssembly, so the archive is a static site. Site | HN discussion

C++26 Is Done, Again - Contracts Ballot Failed Unanimity First Time

The ISO C++ committee completed technical work on C++26 for a second time, in Búzios, Brazil, after the first Draft International Standard ballot failed to pass unanimously - overwhelmingly over contracts: pre, post and contract_assert. An updated document now heads back for a second approval ballot. Roughly 220 experts attended, 35% of them remotely, partly because a US aviation ground stop disrupted travel, formally representing 27 nations. C++29 work continued regardless: break label, continue label, std::ranges::any_view and views::slice were adopted. Next full meeting is Istanbul in March. Herb Sutter | HN discussion

Kagi Mail Opens Public Beta at $99/Year, Built on Stalwart and Open Standards

Kagi opened Kagi Mail to public beta: a @kagimail.com address or your own domains, 50 GB of storage, unlimited aliases and domains, a calendar included, $99 a year after a 60-day trial and bundled with Kagi Ultimate. The stack is boring on purpose - Stalwart server, IMAP, JMAP, CalDAV - so Apple Mail and Thunderbird configure automatically, and there is an MCP server with read access plus Sieve rules that involve no AI at all. Tracking pixels are blocked or proxied and unknown senders are held for approval. Native mobile apps are not expected until Q3 2027. Kagi | HN discussion
