## AI & ML News

### Lean Community Certifies Alpöge's Riemann Hypothesis Proof - 17 Million Lines, Written With Claude
Levent Alpöge released a proof of the Riemann Hypothesis on 20 November as a Lean library 17 million lines long, built with Claude. Within a day the Lean and Mathlib communities signed off against four tests: the statement matches Mathlib's own RiemannHypothesis definition, the library compiles, two independent tools - comparator and con-leche - confirm it proves that exact statement, and the exported proof passes Lean's kernel plus con-ron and third-party checkers written in other languages. The checks ran on an inert export, so malicious code in the sources could not have helped. Nothing was taken on trust.
[Remarks →](https://alpo.ge/remark.pdf) | [Verification →](https://leanprover-community.github.io/blog/posts/riemann-hypothesis/)

### Two More Millennium-Scale Results Land in the Same 48 Hours - Euler and Navier - Stokes Blowup
Terence Tao logged the week's other results in decreasing order of digestion. Alpöge, Tristan Buckmaster and Matei Coiculescu, with heavy AI assistance, wrote a human-readable proof of finite-time blowup for the unforced incompressible Euler equations in three dimensions, formalized in Lean. Alpöge and Buckmaster then produced the Navier - Stokes version the same way: technically readable, strategy broadly clear, but not yet a properly written proof. Tao says the Lean-only proof's strategy "will take some time" to extract. Expect simplification papers for years.
[Tao →](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2026/11/20/unforced-euler-singularity-unforced-navier-stokes-singularity-and-the-generalized-riemann-hypothesis/) | [Discussion →](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50578358)

### Thinking Machines Ships Inkling 2 Mini - A Looped 26B MoE That Runs Offline on a Laptop
Inkling 2 Mini is a mixture-of-experts model with 26.4B total parameters and 3.4B active, but only 24 physical decoder layers: the middle twelve run three times per token, giving 48 layer visits on shared weights. Total footprint is about 20GB, with most weights trained and deployed natively in NVFP4 and the last four layers in MXFP8. It leads open-weights models under 100B on DeepSWE v1.1 (48.2%) and Vals Index v2.2 (46.5%), supports a 1M-token context, and was taken on a flight, where it built a playable flight simulator in airplane mode. Weights are open.
[Release →](https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/inkling-2-mini/) | [HF writeup →](https://huggingface.co/blog/inkling2mini) | [Discussion →](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50581260)

### LLMs Can Read and Write Executables Byte by Byte From a Hex Dump - No Disassembler, No Compiler
George Xenos and Sotiris Giannopoulos gave GPT-6.1 Sol only an xxd hex dump, with every tool stripped out by a local relay, and one reply per task. It identified all four binaries, including a stripped, statically linked ministat whose strings had their high bits set - then found a real upstream bug from the bytes alone: the option labelled 99.5% confidence uses critical values for roughly 99.8%. It patched three binaries in place to add features, all passing hidden tests, and wrote three programs from scratch. DeepSeek V4.1 Flash reached the same malware verdict.
[Paper-style writeup →](https://sgiannopoulos.github.io/blog/machine-code-programming/) | [Discussion →](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50546587)

### Hugging Face Shows Harness Adapters: Same Qwen3.8-27B, 39% → 55% on Terminal-Bench 2.1
Hugging Face trained one LoRA per agent harness for Qwen3.8-27B, with RL running inside the shipped harness - Pi 1.5.2, OpenCode 2.2.7 and Hermes Agent v0.22.6 - rather than a reimplementation of the agent loop. Under pi, the adapter lifted Terminal-Bench 2.1 from 39% to 55% pass@1 while average tokens per task fell from about 2.9K to 1.5K. The three adapters also merge into one super adapter that runs unchanged under all three harnesses. Datasets, rollouts and training all happened on the Hub using TRL's AsyncGRPOTrainer, OpenEnv and Harbor.
[Blog →](https://huggingface.co/blog/harness-adapters-at-scale)

### EverMind-AI/EverOS - One Portable Memory Layer for Every Agent, Local-First and Markdown-Native
Every agent you run keeps its own memory, and none of them talk. That combination is the interesting part - Markdown storage means your notes stay readable and diffable rather than trapped in a vector store, and local-first means the memory survives the vendor. Tagged agent-memory, agentic-ai, chats and clawdbot, at 13,963 stars over 12.8 months, it is the most quietly useful thing in this batch.

### His Monitor Was Cutting Download Speed by 75% - The Culprit Was USB Burst Size
A 2.5-gig symmetrical line was delivering 600 Mbps down and 2.4 Gbps up. Oscar Molnar eventually traced it to a UGREEN 2.5G adapter plugged into a Studio Display XDR: direct to the Mac it hit 2.3 Gbps, behind the display it collapsed. Pings failed 10% of the time with no dropped-frame errors in macOS logs, because the adapter was dropping frames before the host knew. Its counters showed handover flattening at about 168,000 frames per second. Realtek's own driver nearly doubled throughput, flow control reached 2 Gbps, and a newer RTL8157 adapter with larger USB bursts fixed it properly.
[Writeup →](https://blog.tymscar.com/posts/studiodisplayethernet/) | [Discussion →](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50587875)

### The Army Is Going to Detonate 10,000 Pounds of Lead Azide Where It Sits
Derek Lowe's explainer is the best thing written on this. The Army has 68 drums - 9,500 to 10,000 pounds - of lead azide, a primary explosive, at an Iowa ammunition plant, stored about 20 years submerged in an ethanol/water mix. It partially dissolved and recrystallized, making it more shock-sensitive than ever. DoD declared it unserviceable hazardous waste; the EPA issued an emergency permit to blow it in place, on a 19,000 cubic-yard lime-fill foundation with 300 sand-filled barriers aimed south and southwest. A court stay has pushed it to Monday at least.
[Science →](https://www.science.org/content/blog-post/detonating-huge-pile-lead-azide-really) | [Discussion →](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50588982)

### A Pill Made Pancreatic Tumors Undetectable in One in Twelve Patients
In a 61-patient trial of daraxonrasib, all with recently diagnosed advanced pancreatic cancer that had spread, tumours stopped growing in about 90%, shrank or disappeared in just over 60%, and became undetectable on CT in roughly 8% - one in twelve. At one year, 76.6% of patients were alive, against the usual 40 - 45% on chemotherapy alone. Dr Wungki Park of Memorial Sloan Kettering, presenting at the EORTC-NCI-AACR symposium in Barcelona, called it "the closest to cure." It is not a cure, and duration of benefit is unknown.
[Guardian →](https://www.theguardian.com/society/2026/nov/20/pill-pancreatic-cancer-breakthrough-daraxonrasib) | [Discussion →](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50582069)
