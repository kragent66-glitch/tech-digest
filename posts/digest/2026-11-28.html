Credit card skimming scripts using WebRTC found on popular shopping sites

Security researchers discovered active WebRTC-based skimming scripts on several high-traffic e-commerce sites, redirecting users to malicious endpoints that capture payment card data. The scripts leverage browser APIs to establish peer connections without user consent, allowing attackers to intercept sensitive form submissions. Sites affected range from major retailers to smaller marketplaces, with the attacks active since late November 2026. The discovery comes amid growing concerns about browser-based vulnerabilities and the need for stricter WebRTC protections. Security teams are racing to patch the exploits while investigators trace the attack infrastructure and potential affiliate networks involved in distributing the malicious code.

An AI system based on GPT-6.2 Astra identified the location of a buried 17th-century time capsule in a small European village, resolving a decades-long mystery. Google's DeepCrossAttention achieves comparable model quality up to 3x faster

Google researchers introduced DeepCrossAttention, a transformer architecture modification that accelerates attention computations by up to three times while maintaining model quality. The technique restructures residual connections in transformer blocks, allowing for more efficient parallel processing of long sequences without sacrificing accuracy on standard benchmarks. Early benchmarks show improvements on GLUE, SQuAD, and code completion tasks, with the largest gains seen in long-context applications. Google plans to integrate the technique into future model releases and has open-sourced the core implementation under an Apache 2.0 license. The work contributes to ongoing efforts to reduce compute costs and latency in transformer-based systems, which remain energy-intensive to train and deploy.

False computer-checked proofs

Cryptographer Daniel J. Bernstein released a technical analysis demonstrating how computers can be tricked into accepting incorrect proofs through carefully crafted counterexamples. The work builds on prior research into "relaxed correctness" in automated verification tools and introduces a framework for systematically generating false positives. Bernstein argues that formal verification pipelines remain vulnerable to subtle model assumptions that can be exploited, particularly in high-assurance systems like cryptography and safety-critical software. The paper includes several worked examples and suggests new verification techniques to detect and filter out such attacks. The work has sparked debate in the formal verification community about whether current assurance levels are sufficient for critical systems.

First images of the impact of DART asteroid deflection experiment

Europe's Hera mission has returned the first detailed images of the DART asteroid deflection experiment impact site, revealing a crater with visible ejecta rays and a central peak structure. The images, captured at resolutions up to 1.8 meters per pixel, show how the collision altered Dimorphos' surface and confirm the expected momentum transfer effects. Scientists are using the data to validate simulation models and refine plans for future planetary defense missions. The HERA mission was launched in October 2026 to study the impact and gather detailed measurements of the resulting asteroid deformation. The images are publicly available through ESA's data portal and have been widely shared in scientific and public media.
