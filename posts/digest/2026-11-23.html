AI & ML News

Google Prices Gemini 4 Neon at $3/$15 With a 61 on the Artificial Analysis Index

Gemini 4 Neon shipped on 18 November as Google's fast frontier tier, priced at $3 per million input tokens, $0.15 per million cached input - a 95% discount - and $15 per million output, with a 1M-token context window. At its xhigh thinking level it scores 61 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, third overall and behind only larger, slower models, plus second on the Vals Index at 70.9% and first on both the Legal Research Bench and Tax Agent Bench. It also takes first on Zapier's AutomationBench at 56.3% and Andon Labs' Vending-Bench 2 at $16,843. Koray Kavukcuoglu framed the release around production code: 65.2% on Terminal-Bench 4.1, 58.6 on the coding agent index. DeepMind

OpenAI Publishes Its Own Perplexity Curve and Proposes an Industry "Common Limit"

In a post arguing AI progress is best tracked as compression, OpenAI published internal log-perplexity figures for the first time: GPT-3.5 sat near 0.99, GPT-4 around 0.91, and current models are below 0.8. OpenAI says a model at roughly 0.76 began producing groundbreaking maths results, and it expects another capability jump in the 0.68 - 0.7 window. The proposal is policy, not product - perplexity as a shared cross-lab metric, plus "common limits": thresholds developers commit not to cross until something like a successor to chain-of-thought monitors exists. OpenAI concedes the metric compares badly across labs, ignores test-time reasoning, and has an unknown floor. OpenAI

DeepMind's Agents Formally Verified Signal's Protocol in Four Weeks, Generating Six Million Lines of Lean

AlphaProof Code, detailed on 10 November, is a multi-LLM agentic framework that splits verification the way a research team would: human experts decide what to prove, a group of agents does the proving. Agents powered by Gemini 4 Carbon generated over six million lines of Lean, and because Lean machine-checks everything, reviewers only had to inspect about one percent of the codebase. The proofs cover compiled code rather than protocol design - the gap where real vulnerabilities hide. Two previously fixed bugs, reintroduced, both produced machine-checked proofs of insecurity. Four weeks, against the years such work normally takes. Signal CTO Ehren Kret called it a major breakthrough; the Lean artifacts are open-sourced. DeepMind

87% of Anthropic's 2028 US Power Will Come From Gas and 11% From Coal

The Better Data Center Project modelled Anthropic's 15 GW of contracted compute, of which 5.9 - 9.6 GW is tied to specific US sites, and estimates about 87% of its power will come from gas and 11% from coal in 2028. Anthropic held no direct US renewable commitments as of November 2026 - its disclosed renewables sit in Norway and Canada - while buying commitments for 1.7 GW of heavy-duty gas generators for $3.4bn. Mid-case emissions reach 32 million tonnes CO2e, more than California's entire power sector emitted in 2024. Carbon intensity runs 1,033 - 1,076 lb CO2e/MWh against a 767 US average, and 80% of on-site capacity is inefficient simple-cycle plant. Better Data Center Project | HN

180B Parameters on a 32GB Mac: Expert Streaming Lands Within 6% of Strata

Tarek Ziadé's Hugging Face writeup and Transformers PR #50717 add an experts_offload path that streams MoE experts from SSD into a preallocated slot pool, remapping router IDs so the existing grouped_mm kernel runs unchanged. Qwen3.8-Flash-Next - 48 layers of 512 experts, 10 active, 121GB in FP8, plus a 51GB n-gram table - then runs in about 18GiB on an M5, peaking at 25.4GiB on a 6K prompt. Decode lands at 10.0 tok/s against Strata's 14.8, prefill at 68 against 220. On Qwen3.6-35B-A3B with one A10G, vanilla Transformers plus Accelerate managed 0.16 tok/s where llama.cpp hit 63 - 64. Hugging Face

Hidden Gems

superdesigndev/treg - OpenRouter for agent tools, 2,034 stars a month

treg pitches itself as "OpenRouter for agent tools" - one CLI-mediated layer for the API keys and credentials agents need, instead of every agent holding its own. The topics line reads agents, api-keys, credentials and developer-tools, so the shape is a broker sitting in front of the tools an agent calls, with keys kept out of each agent's own config. Traction is the story: 8,753 stars in 4.3 months, a velocity of roughly 2,034 stars a month, and a push inside the last day. That pace puts it among the fastest-growing developer tools on GitHub this quarter, and the credential-broker angle is why. GitHub

alibaba/zvec - An in-process vector database with full-text search alongside it

Alibaba's zvec is a lightweight, in-process vector database - embedded, not client-server, so it links into your process instead of adding a service to operate. The topic list is broader than most vector stores: faiss for the approximate-nearest-neighbour lineage, fts for full-text search, plus db and embedded for the deployment model. That combination points at hybrid retrieval inside a single binary. It carries 16,280 stars at 11.6 months old, a velocity of 1,404 stars a month, and a push within the last day. Embedded vector search is crowded; an embedded full-text index beside it is what makes this one worth a look. GitHub

crynta/terax-ai - A 7MB terminal-first AI-native development environment

terax-ai is a 7MB terminal-first development environment with AI built into the editor rather than bolted on through a plugin. Its topics point at a deliberately thin stack: libghostty for the terminal, linux as the primary target, agents and code-editor for the interaction model. Seven megabytes is the entire pitch - a native binary where comparable editors ship Electron and several hundred megabytes. It has 9,488 stars in 7.1 months, roughly 1,337 a month, last pushed two days ago. Whether the editor survives contact with large real-world projects is untested, but the size constraint is doing genuine architectural work. GitHub

Nasiko-Labs/nasiko - An open agent runtime where security is a first-class topic

Nasiko bills itself as the open runtime for AI agents, and its topics separate it from the pile of agent frameworks: agent-runtime, agent-security, cli, hacktoberfest. Runtime plus security in one project suggests the interesting surface is what an agent is permitted to do - isolation, tool permissions, audit trail - rather than another prompt-orchestration DSL. It has 11,939 stars at 9.3 months, 1,280 a month, with a push five days back. As sandboxing becomes the bottleneck for shipping agents into production, an open runtime that names security as a concern is the layer most enterprises are still missing. GitHub

pixel-agents-hq/pixel-agents - Manage your AI workforce as pixel art

pixel-agents describes itself as a playful way to manage your AI workforce, rendering running coding agents as pixel-art characters, with claude-code and developer-tools among its topics. Under the novelty it is an observability surface: agents are long-running, parallel and opaque, and a visual floor plan of who is doing what is a cheaper mental model than tailing logs in six terminals. It has 10,335 stars in 9.5 months, 1,092 a month, pushed three days ago. It is the kind of project that gets dismissed as a toy and then quietly becomes the dashboard left open on a second monitor. GitHub

StarTrail-org/LEANN - RAG on your own laptop with 97% less storage

LEANN is a RAG index that keeps 97% less on disk than conventional vector storage while running entirely on-device, per its own description, and it is built on work that took a best paper at MLsys 2026. Its topics span faiss, llama-index, langchain and gpt-oss, so it is designed to sit under existing retrieval stacks rather than replace them, and privacy is the point: nothing leaves the machine. It has 13,810 stars at 17.5 months, 790 a month, last pushed three days ago. Storage, not latency, is what usually kills a personal RAG setup; a 97% cut changes what fits on a laptop. GitHub

Under-the-Radar Finds

apache/maka - An Apache-incubating agent workspace that keeps a record of everything it did

Apache Maka (Incubating) is a high-performance agent workspace whose stated differentiator is a complete record of everything the agent did - a transcript deep enough to audit, replay, or hand to a reviewer. Apache incubation means the governance is donated to the foundation rather than a vendor's roadmap, which matters if you need an agent runtime you can fork and keep. It has 5,998 stars at 5.9 months, about 1,014 a month, pushed today. The agent-runtime space is crowded with startups; an Apache-licensed entrant with auditability as the headline feature is a different bet. GitHub

zeronsh/zeron - One native control plane for Claude Code, Codex, Cursor and Devin

zeron is a native control plane for coding agents - Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, Devin and others - sitting above them instead of inside any single one. If you run several agents across repos, the missing piece is rarely another model: it is scheduling, session state, and knowing which agent touched what. Its topics name coding-agent and codex alongside claude-code and cursor, so cross-vendor coverage is the explicit goal. It has 3,734 stars in 4.1 months, 902 a month, pushed today. Small, young, and aimed at the exact seam where multi-agent workflows currently fall apart. GitHub

ZSeven-W/openpencil - An open vector design tool built around concurrent Agent Teams

openpencil calls itself the first open-source AI-native vector design tool, but the more interesting claim is the second one: concurrent Agent Teams, several agents editing the same live canvas at once rather than taking turns. Design-as-Code is the framing - prompts become UI on the canvas, not a mockup handed back in a chat pane. Its topics list agent, agent-team, claude and claude-code. It has 7,322 stars in 9.2 months, 799 a month, pushed yesterday. Concurrent multi-agent editing of one artefact is the hard version of agent collaboration; most tools still serialise it. GitHub

Pinvou/pinvou-agent - A desktop agent aimed at real deliverables, built on ACP and agent skills

pinvou-agent is an open-source desktop agent for tools, files, knowledge and workflows, with real deliverables as the stated output rather than conversation. Two topics are worth noting: agent-client-protocol, the emerging ACP layer, and agent-skills, the reusable-capability model that Claude Code popularised - so it is building on standards instead of inventing another plugin format. It has 3,044 stars at 4.0 months, 760 a month, pushed today. Desktop agents are where filesystem and credential access actually live, which also makes them the hardest to get right; ACP support is the part to watch. GitHub

Fun Tech

A Once-Daily Pill Doubled Survival in Advanced Pancreatic Cancer, and First-Line Data Looks Stronger

Daraxonrasib, sold as Rasonque, is the first drug to successfully target the RAS mutations that drive more than 90% of pancreatic cancers. In the 500-patient phase 3 RASolute 302 trial in previously treated metastatic disease, median overall survival was 13.2 months against 6.7 on chemotherapy, progression-free survival 7.2 against 3.6 months, and treatment stopped for side effects in 1.2% versus 11.2%. At the ENA symposium in Barcelona on 20 November, first-line data from 61 patients combining it with gemcitabine and nab-paclitaxel showed a 60.7% confirmed response rate and median progression-free survival of 12.8 months; the Guardian reports one in 12 patients had no detectable cancer. Guardian | HN

Toyota, Nissan and Honda Will Standardise Steel Specs, Plastics and Wiring Colours From Next Year

Japan's three largest automakers agreed to standardise parts, announced in Tokyo by JAMA chairman Koji Sato, who is also Toyota's vice chairman. They start narrow and unglamorous: unified quality criteria for steel, consistent protocols for plastics and resins, and a universal colour-coding scheme for wiring harnesses - picked because consumers never notice them, brand identity survives, and all three carry significant production cost. Talks begin with domestic suppliers, with international ones to follow, and the first standards land next year. The motive is defensive: seven Japanese automakers, including Mazda, Subaru, Mitsubishi and Suzuki, are trying to hold ground against Chinese competitors and want the savings to fund development. Car and Driver | HN

GTA 6 Ships 60 Bird Species, and an Audubon Writer Ticked the Whole List in Two Days

Grand Theft Auto VI earned more than $1.8 billion on its first day, and buried inside Vice City is a working birding sim: 60 species in the Progress compendium, alongside 338 vehicles and 205 weapons, though pre-release marketing promised 62. Audubon's writer used the in-game binoculars' "study" action to log all 60 across two hours after midnight plus six more the next day, finishing with an American Barn Owl on a water tank. All 60 are available from the start, with no story gating. The ecology is partly right, from mangrove Keys to grassy swamp, except that Whooping Cranes are absurdly common. There is also a zoo. Audubon | HN

Spelunkr Runs 37 Cable Channels Off the Internet Archive's Forgotten Tapes

Spelunkr is a free alternative TV network with 37 channels and 4,360 hours of programming, all of it pulled from the Internet Archive's forgotten videos and scheduled like cable. Channel 2 is "Saturday Morning", running a five-hour CN Saturday Crushzone block from September 2009; channel 9 is Unsolved Mysteries episodes from 1992, 1994 and 1999. Everything is live-synced 24/7, with no account, no ads and no checkout - the site notes it checked. Suggest a channel and a person reads it; tell them a tape is broken and they fix it. It is the warmest use of the Archive's long tail yet. Spelunkr | HN

Small SF Is a Miniature, Live San Francisco With 243 Buses and Trains Moving Right Now

smallsf.com is an illustrated isometric map of San Francisco driven by live transit data: 243 buses and trains moving at any given moment, boats on the bay, planes overhead, simulated street traffic, and a weather line reading 53 degrees and overcast with light wind from the north. Landmarks are hand-placed - Sutro Tower, the Ferry Building, Coit Tower, Oracle Park, the Castro Theatre, Mission Dolores Park - and layers toggle transit, boats and planes independently. There is no account and no app install. It is a webpage that behaves like a model railway, and it makes the city legible at a glance rather than through a route planner. Small SF | HN
