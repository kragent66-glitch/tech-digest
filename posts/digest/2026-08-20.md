# Mojo goes open source, a 27B model matches the giants, and companies start rationing AI tokens

The day's most important technology news, curated with context: Mojo finally goes open source, Qwen's 27B model punches far above its weight, an AirTag exposes how Amazon sources AI training data, and leaked Accenture audio reveals the "tokenpocalypse" inside enterprises.

**Mojo is now open source.** Modular released the Mojo compiler and toolchain under an Apache 2 license, weeks after shipping Mojo 1.0. The language was promised as a Python superset in May 2023, then officially pivoted away from that goal around August 2025: "It's okay if it doesn't" remain a superset, the team said. The compiler's job now is making GPU programming as painless as possible with Python-inspired syntax. Apache 2 means commercial use with no copyleft strings attached - the same license that powers most of the modern AI stack.

**Qwen 3.8 27B scores 52 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index.** That equals GPT-5.6 Luna (max) and sits one point behind GLM-5.2 (max) and DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (max). The catch: those rivals are 753B and 1.7T parameters respectively. A 27B model matching that bar is significant for anyone who wants capable AI on a laptop or a modest server, and Qwen keeps shipping these under Apache 2.

**An AirTag caught an Amazon facility scanning rare books.** 404 Media tracked a ~1,000-book order from a suspicious bulk buyer and found it delivered to Amazon's LAS8 facility in Las Vegas, where the VGT3 corner runs destructive book scanning. Amazon workers confirmed the practice in forum posts. It is the latest evidence of AI-training data sourcing running far outside public view - and a reminder that the "licensed data" debate is not just about the big AI labs.

**The tokenpocalypse is here, according to Accenture.** 404 Media reported on leaked Accenture meeting audio where the firm's own internal data shows non-engineers now drive token consumption. An agentic AI strategy lead named PDF-to-markdown conversion as one of the biggest token chewers - prompting a colleague to joke about exactly that workflow. Companies are visibly scrambling to control AI spend, which explains the industry-wide push toward cheaper models, caching, and local inference.

**Dario Amodei on why people distrust AI.** In a rare on-record comment, the Anthropic CEO argued the tech industry faces a fundamental crisis of trust: "saying that AI will cure cancer is more a cliche than it is inspiring." His prescription is blunt - the fix is actually delivering on promises, not better messaging.

**Platform note: LinkedIn still blocks document posts via its public API.** Testing confirms that the self-serve API rejects document content (PDF carousels) with a 422 union error - document posts require partner access. If you have been wondering why tool vendors cannot post PDFs for you, that is why. Text and single-image posts work fine.

**Why it matters.** Three threads worth watching: open-source licensing keeps consolidating the AI stack (Apache 2 is now the default language of the industry), small models are closing the gap faster than anyone expected, and the tokenpocalypse is quietly reshaping enterprise buying - which will feed back into pricing and model design for the rest of the year.
